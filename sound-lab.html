🎧 Meadow Sound Lab

A quiet sound palette for the meadow, made on the fly with no audio files. Every pitched sound uses one pentatonic scale. Rabbits play a high kalimba, foxes a low wooden marimba, and the meadow itself rings a soft bell. Now and then a short felt-piano piece drifts in, like the music in Minecraft.

🔊

"Let the meadow play" runs a 20-second day like the game at Calm speed and triggers events about as often as a real meadow does.

Birds sing by day with a dawn chorus. Crickets come out at night, and on spring nights, while there's frogspawn in the shallows, a frog purrs now and then. The wind comes in gusts that swell, cross from one side to the other and rustle the grass and the leaves of the trees on screen (crisp and dry in autumn, nearly bare in winter); in winter and storms a hard gust whistles faintly, in tune. Winter has a few snowy chimes, and a wind chime rings over it all, more often the more rabbits there are (and in gusts). Rain and storms hiss and drip, fog and snow muffle everything, a heatwave brings cicadas. Bees flying on screen add a faint hum in tune. Fast, day and night blur into one steady mix, and only the big moments (seasons, newcomers, extinctions) still make a sound.

How it's put together

  • One scale, three voices. D major pentatonic only, so any two sounds that overlap still sound nice together.
  • Rising means life, falling means loss. Love and births go up. Catches and deaths go down, softly, with no stings or screams.
  • Quiet by default. Each sound has a cooldown, and there are at most about 6 animal sounds a second. Sounds far from the camera come out quieter and with more echo.
  • Music now and then. A short felt-piano piece every 5 to 10 minutes, then just the meadow again. In keys that hold the pentatonic, so it sits under everything else, with one chord borrowed from outside for its one moment.
  • The clock is the score. The background follows the time of day, the season and the weather, so a year in the meadow has its own sound.
