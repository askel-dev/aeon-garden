Round 2: five ideas

Each sketch is built on one finding about what makes a near-human thing unsettling, and stays a fairy tale, not horror:

  • Almost right beats very wrong. Unease comes from a mix of human and not-human cues (perceptual mismatch, MacDorman); a pile of monster features just reads as "monster". So each has one or two things off, by a little.
  • Relations, not parts. Proportions and joints set slightly wrong (configural processing) unsettle more than an odd feature does.
  • Not knowing. Creepiness is uncertainty about whether there's a threat at all (McAndrew & Koehnke). No face to read, a pose with no clear intent, can't tell if it faces you, can't tell if it's a tree.
  • Still among moving things, as a corpse is the floor of Mori's valley; and movement deepens the valley, so "It notices you" tries one slow head turn and nothing else.
  • Something wearing a familiar shape (the folk mimics): a doe, a sapling, the forager's own hood.
