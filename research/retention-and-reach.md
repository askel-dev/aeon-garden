# Retention and reach: research notes for Nobody's Meadow

2026-10-12. Cut short by time: sections 1 and 2 are researched; 3 and 4 are marked UNFINISHED and rest on
general knowledge, not fresh sources. Sources are listed at the end of each section.

## 1. Why people come back to idle, ambient and companion games

**Rusty's Retirement** (Mister Morris Games, April 2024). A farm that sits as a strip along the bottom of your
desktop while you work. About 330k Steam copies in its first months (GameDiscoverCo), later 500k+ (Wikipedia).
$7, about $5 net a copy; a $4 supporter DLC sold to 11% of buyers. About 140k wishlists at launch. Released the
Friday before Steam's Farming Fest, which gave it two peaks (about 20k copies a day for 5 days). 26% of sales from
China, 10% Japan, 6% Korea, 4% Taiwan, with 11 languages at launch. Twitch viewers could spawn on the farm. 20-25%
discounts set off Steam's wishlist emails, each a bump. Its hook says itself in one line ("works while you work").
Nothing bad happens while you're away. It started as a 2-week prototype. Steam has since made "Desktop
Companion" an official tag, and there's an idle-themed Steam fest (Idler Fest); Bongo Cat rose through it.

**Neko Atsume** (Hit-Point). You put out food and toys, close the app, and come back to see who visited. Cats leave
fish (the currency) and now and then a gift (a memento). You never see the cats arrive: the reward is the
surprise on opening. Collecting (a cat book of who has visited) gives the check-in a reason. Hardly marketed,
lightly monetised.

**Animal Crossing.** Runs on the real clock and calendar. Eguchi (GDC 2006, as reported) said that inconvenience
makes much of its pull: the shop's poor stock and the clock make things scarce, so there's always a reason for
tomorrow, but there are also things to do at once so nobody is frustrated. Shared moments (KK Slider's Saturday
show) and visiting friends' towns give people something to talk about.

**Viridi** (succulents in a pot, real time). Growth so slow it's a desk plant you check in on; plants get thirsty,
forgivingly; you can name a favourite, it may flower for you, and photos make an album of its life. The developers
dropped plants as unlockable goals, because players then chased the goals instead of caring for the plants they
had. A weekly free seedling. Critics: not much to do, every plant just gets bigger.

**Tamagotchi.** It goes on needing you while you're not looking, and that made people feel responsible. The
same death drove people off: a reset is easy, and many didn't bother. Lesson: what happens while you're away
should be legible and recoverable, not a punishment.

**Kind Words** (Popcannibal). Anonymous letters, no visible popularity numbers, heavy moderation; a surprise hit
nobody submitted to shows. People come back for other people, and for a feeling. Its sequel carries over your old
letters (your history is kept).

**Stardew Valley.** A day is about 14 real minutes and ends whether you're done or not; each morning is a fresh set
of choices (the "one more day" pull). Barone said the short day made what to do today matter. Players who feel
rushed sleep early or mod the clock.

**A Short Hike.** Spread through Humble Monthly (April 2019) and the developer's Twitter (short clips), then itch
and Steam the same day (July 2019), IGF grand prize 2020. Small and finished beats big and open.

What this means for the meadow:
- The away card is this game's Neko Atsume moment and its strongest retention tool. Make it a moment of surprise,
  with a picture (who was born, who came, who you named and what became of them), not just a list.
- A collection to fill (who has visited, firsts seen) gives a check-in a reason without a goal. The news and
  Yours are halfway there.
- Naming one animal is the Viridi/Tamagotchi attachment, already built. Its fate should headline the next visit.
- Absence must never punish (Rusty, Viridi). The Dry Year's loss at `?keep` should come while you watch, or be
  shown kindly on return.
- Real-world anchors: a meadow that matches the real season or a weekly event gives a reason to come back on a day.
- "Works while you work": a small window, a corner mode or a desktop build is a genre people already search for.

## 2. How browser simulations spread: Sandspiel and Orb.farm

**Sandspiel** (Max Bittker, December 2018). Hacker News "Sandspiel – A falling sand game built in Rust and WebGL":
1323 points, 185 comments. Its making-of post went round HN again in 2019 (160) and 2023 (74), each time sending
new people. What kept it going: an upload gallery modeled on Powder Game's, where every player had an audience at
once. Creations save as PNGs, people vote, remix culture grew; 70k+ uploads, a couple of thousand players a day.
No accounts (low friction). A Twitter feed of uploads. Bittker found most players use it as a paint program,
the simulation an afterthought, and regretted a bigger canvas many devices can't run at 60 fps. Also listed on
itch and CrazyGames.

**Orb.farm** (Bittker, May 2020). A sealed aquatic ecosystem: sand, algae, daphnia, fish, bacteria; it keeps running
while the tab is closed (Genbeta's hook: "watch it evolve even when you're not there"). Its first HN posts in
May 2020 got 17, 4 and 3 points. Reposted by someone else in May 2023: **783 points**, 101 comments. Same page,
three years later. Boing Boing, The Verge and Destructoid covered it in 2020. HN comments: "mesmerizing", praised it
as a break from work, and complained mostly about balance (daphnia overgraze, grass fills the orb, fish die of low
oxygen at night): people want the ecosystem to be keepable, and puzzle at why it collapses.

**Also seen on HN:** "A sealed garden watered once in 53 years" (412 points): people love self-sustaining
ecosystems. "Show HN: Ecosim, a cellular ecosystem simulator" (150).

What this means for the meadow:
- HN is a lottery; reposting works. Post as Show HN with a one-line title saying what it does, then again with a
  write-up (the making-of post is a second launch, as Sandspiel's was). Good write-up topics already exist:
  painted foxes without sprites, the sim running 2000 ticks a frame, the trees painted in code, the
  terrain lab, keeping a world in IndexedDB.
- A share loop is what kept Sandspiel alive: something a player makes that others can open. The terrain lab's
  drawn link (`drawnToLink`) and `?seed=` links are the seed of a gallery: "my meadow", a still or a short clip
  of a named animal's life, a link that opens that exact meadow.
- Orb.farm's critics wanted to keep the ecosystem alive: the Dry Year at `?keep` answers exactly that wish.
- Runs-while-you're-away was the hook journalists wrote up. The meadow already does this (`AWAY_MIN`, the away
  card); it should be in the pitch.

## 3. Steam port and wishlists (UNFINISHED: not researched this session)

From the Rusty's Retirement numbers above and general knowledge, to be checked:
- About 140k wishlists gave Rusty's a 330k first few months; a small game commonly needs a few thousand wishlists
  to be seen at launch at all. Steam Next Fest demos and themed fests (Idler Fest, Farming Fest, a cozy or
  wildlife fest) are where small games collect them.
- A free web version can work as the demo and the wishlist funnel (Cookie Clicker, Melvor Idle and A Dark Room went
  from browser to paid Steam releases), as long as the Steam version adds something the web lacks.
- What Steam could add here: a desktop-companion window (the tag exists and is searched), achievements, a
  bigger or hand-made valley, the keeper campaign. Electron, Tauri or NW.js wrap the existing page.
- Price against the genre's medians ($0-5 idlers, $10-15 cozy sims; Rusty's chose $7). Localise early: Asia was
  46% of Rusty's sales.

## 4. Onboarding a sim with no goal (UNFINISHED: not researched this session)

From the sources above and general knowledge:
- Viridi: unlockable goals made players chase goals instead of caring. Give small "things to see", not progress.
- Animal Crossing: have something rewarding to do at once, alongside the slow things.
- The game already has the intro, the guide's three steps and TRIES. Still to look at: first-minute data
  (GoatCounter could count how far people get), whether naming an animal is offered early enough, and Terra Nil's
  one-cause-one-effect tutorial (noted in PLAN.md).
